# Video editor

A recording made for editing opens in the video editor. The window has a settings rail on the left (Cursor, Keystrokes, Audio, Camera, Background, Motion Blur), the preview in the middle with playback under it, and the timeline at the bottom: a ruler, a zoom lane, and a clip lane with pictures of the recording. The preview draws each frame from the project through `frameState`, so the pointer is a layer over the untouched `screen.mp4`. Space plays and pauses. The clip lane holds the clips the finished video keeps: Command-B splits at the playhead, Delete removes a clip, dragging a clip's edge trims it, and each cut has a mark above and below the lane. The zoom lane holds zooms on the finished video's timeline: a click adds a 2 s zoom at 200%, a drag adds one over its stretch, edges resize, the body moves, and a right click opens Level, Apply Level to All, Mode, Split at Playhead, Duplicate, Remove Zoom and Remove All Zooms. A picked zoom takes the settings panel over. The camera moves in and out over 0.78 s on smootherstep, and zooms less than 1.56 s apart hand over without returning to 100%. Full Screen Preview hides the settings and the timeline. Closing the window writes changed edits to `project.json`. Export, top right, renders the edited video through the same `frameState` with VideoToolbox's hardware H.264 encoder and lists it in History with a Quick Access card.

## Sub-features

- `editor-open` opens a project folder from the Quick Access card's Edit, History's Edit, or `edit-recording?path=<folder>`. A plain `.mp4` keeps Play.
- `editor-layout` is the rail, the panel, the preview, the playback row, and the timeline lanes.
- `editor-sections` switches the panel. Cursor has Show cursor, Size, Style (White, Black, Dot), Motion (Natural, Smooth), Hide when not moving and Hide when typing; Background has its own settings; the others say what the recording holds.
- `editor-cursor` is how those settings draw the pointer. Smooth runs the recorded path through a critically damped spring. Hide when not moving fades the pointer 1.5 s into a rest. Hide when typing fades it at a key press without Command or Control until it next moves. Command-Z and Shift-Command-Z undo and redo each change, and one slider drag is one step.
- `editor-background` puts the recording on a background: None, gradients with Show more and Show less, wallpapers with Add image, and plain colours, then Padding, Shadow and Corners under them. Padding grows the video on every side, so the export panel's sizes grow with it. An added image is copied into the project folder. Every change is one undo step, a slider drag included.
- `editor-playback` is Space, Go to Start, Go to End, clicking or dragging the timeline, arrow keys on the focused timeline, and the volume, which is off for a recording without sound.
- `editor-clips` is splitting, deleting and trimming. Command-B or the scissors button splits the clip under the playhead. A click picks a clip; Delete or the trash button removes it, or the clip under the playhead when none is picked, and a video keeps at least one. A drag on a clip's edge trims it on the source's frame grid, and the playhead rides the edge. On the focused timeline Option-Left and Option-Right move the clip's start, Option-Command its end, Shift a second at a time. Command-Z and Shift-Command-Z undo and redo every edit, a drag or a run of key presses as one step. `project.json` stores the clips as `edits.clips`; an older file without them keeps the whole recording. Export writes only the clips.
- `editor-fullscreen` is Full Screen Preview. Esc or the same button returns.
- `editor-save` writes changed edits to `project.json` on close and never touches `screen.mp4`.
- `editor-export` is Export: the panel (Resolution, Quality, the size estimate), the progress with Cancel, and the export's card with Copy, Save, Play and Close. Export saves changed edits first. A cancelled or failed export leaves no file. The export lives in its History entry's folder until Save copies it to the save folder.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Record for Editing, then choose **Edit** on the recording's Quick Access card.
- Select the recording in Capture History and choose **Edit**, or double-click it.
- Open `cornermark-dev://edit-recording?path=<project folder>`.

## Driving it with link.sh

Preconditions:

- The baseline in [the map](./README.md) holds.
- The instance has a project folder. Record one from the synthetic display as [Recording](./recording.md) describes.

- **Open by link.** Run `$V/link.sh edit-recording "<root>/saved/Cornermark Recording <date>"`. The output has `video editor opened project=<name> seconds=<s> size=<w>x<h> pointerSamples=<n>`, then `present window=<name> visible=false appActive=false`, and `"onScreenWindowsMax":0`. A folder outside the root is copied to `<root>/import/` first.
- **Export by link.** Run `$V/link.sh edit-recording "<project folder>" export`. It sends `edit-recording?path=<folder>&export=1`, which opens the editor and exports with the default choices. The log has `video export started ... estimatedBytes=` and then `video exported ... frames= seconds= projectSeconds= bytes= hardware=true encoder=com.apple.videotoolbox.videoencoder.ave.avc renderSeconds=`. The file is in `<root>/History/<entry>/`, and `<root>/History/.incoming` is empty afterwards.
- **Refusal.** A folder without `project.json` is refused by `link.sh` before anything is sent.
- **Everything else.** The window is built but never drawn in the instance, so the rest is harness-only. Run `swift run HarnessShell` and read the sections `video editor: opening`, `video editor: layout and sections`, `video editor: playback`, `video editor: full screen preview`, `video editor: edits and the project file`, `video editor: cursor size, style, motion and hiding`, `video editor: split, delete and trim`, `video editor: preview frame time`, and `video editor: playing across a cut`. The `CUT` line says how far the live player was from the playhead after a cut. Snapshots of the clips are `video-editor-clips-split.png`, `video-editor-clips-selected.png` and `video-editor-clips-selected-dark.png`. Snapshots are `build/harness/shell/video-editor.png`, `video-editor-dark.png`, and `video-editor-min.png`. The Cursor panel is in `video-editor-cursor.png`, `video-editor-cursor-dark.png` and `video-editor-cursor-off.png`.
- **Export, harness.** `swift run HarnessShell` sections `video export: panel`, `progress and the file`, `size estimate`, `Quick Access card`, `the file matches the preview`, `a trimmed project`, `cancel`, `closing the window` and `by link`. `EXPORT`, `ESTIMATE`, `PARITY`, `TRIMMED` and `CUTFRAME` lines carry the numbers; `CUTFRAME` compares the file's frame on each side of a cut with the source frame the clips name and with the frame the cut removed. Snapshots are `video-export-panel.png`, `video-export-panel-dark.png`, `video-export-progress.png` and `parity-<t>-preview|drawn|file.png`.
- **Cursor parity.** The section `video export: cursor style, motion and hiding match the preview` exports a project whose pointer moves, rests and is typed over, with two cursor choices. `PARITYFRAMES` says how many of its frames the preview drew differently from the export, and `PARITY cursor` lines compare pixels where the spring trails, where typing fades the pointer, and where it is gone.
- **Film the cursor.** `CORNERMARK_CURSOR_FILM=<folder> swift run HarnessShell` gives a fixture project a scripted pointer track with a shaking hand, rests and a typed word, and films the real editor offscreen into `C1.mp4`, `C2.mp4`, `C3.mp4`, `C4.mp4`, `C5.mp4` and `C7.mp4`. The orange trails in C4 and C5 are drawn by the harness.
- **Film export.** `CORNERMARK_EXPORT_FILM=<folder> swift run HarnessShell` films the real editor, export panel and card offscreen into `X1.mp4`, `X2.mp4`, `X3.mp4` and `X6.mp4`, next to frames read back from the exported files. The export runs at its own speed while the film samples it.
- **Film the real window.** With the screen unlocked, `$V/link.sh record/test-window editor "<project folder>" E5` opens the editor for the folder as the instance's test window, behind every other window and without focus, and logs `test window id=<n> ... adopted=<name>`. Then `$V/link.sh record "window=<n>&style=video"` records it. Once that recording runs, the editor plays scene `E5` or `E6` from scripted input sent straight to its views (`film step ...` lines), stops the recording itself, and closes the editor (`film E5 done clips=...`). Run `$V/watch.sh` around it. The first run at 01:41 failed the focus rule: a system alert from UserNotificationCenter came to the front as the recording started, and the instance was frontmost at least once, cause not known. Read `focus.jsonl` and the WindowServer log before you run it again.
- **Background, harness.** `swift run HarnessShell` sections `video editor: background` and `video export: the file matches the preview on a gradient|wallpaper|image background`. `PARITY <fill>` lines carry the numbers. Snapshots are `video-background.png`, `video-background-dark.png`, `video-background-more.png` and `video-background-image.png`.
- **Film the background.** `CORNERMARK_BACKGROUND_FILM=<folder> swift run HarnessShell` films the real editor offscreen into `B1.mp4` (each kind of fill) and `B2.mp4` (the three sliders, undo, and a frame read back from the export). It sends no key events.
- **Film trimming.** `CORNERMARK_TRIM_FILM=<folder> swift run HarnessShell` films the real editor offscreen into `E5.mp4` (split, delete, edge drags, undo and redo) and `E6.mp4` (cut marks, then the trimmed export's frames on each side of every cut, measured against the source).
- **Zooms, harness.** `swift run HarnessShell` sections `video editor: zooms on the timeline`, `a zoom's menu`, `the zoom panel`, `zooms from the keyboard`, `the preview zooms`, `zoomed frames in the file match the preview` and `zooms are saved`. `PARITY zoom` lines compare the preview with the export through the moves. Snapshots are `video-zoom.png` and `video-zoom-dark.png`.
- **Film zooms.** `CORNERMARK_ZOOM_FILM=<folder> swift run HarnessShell` films the real editor offscreen into `Z1.mp4`, `Z2.mp4`, `Z3.mp4`, `Z4.mp4`, `Z6-Z7.mp4` and `Z14.mp4`. A context menu is not drawn offscreen, so the film draws the real menu's items in a box it labels as the harness's.
- **Measure the curve.** `CORNERMARK_ZOOM_CURVE=<folder holding screen.mp4> CORNERMARK_ZOOMS="1-4@2,5.6-8@3" swift run HarnessShell` exports that video with those zooms to `<folder>/zoomed.mp4` for a frame-by-frame scale measurement. Use a static, textured synthetic video.
- **Film it.** `CORNERMARK_EDITOR_FILM=<folder> swift run HarnessShell` records a project from the fixture display, opens it the way a user would, and films the real editor offscreen into `E2.mp4`, `E3.mp4`, `E4.mp4`, and `E10.mp4`. The harness decodes the source on a scripted clock so playback runs at its own speed.

## Gotchas

- The instance never orders the editor in, so `visible=false` is correct. A `visible=true` line means isolation is broken.
- The preview's picture and pointer are Core Animation layers. `cacheDisplay` leaves them out, so a snapshot of the window renders the preview's layer tree on top. `editorImage` in `ChecksVideoEditor.swift` does that.
- An offscreen window cannot enter macOS full screen. The harness checks the layout and the dark appearance of Full Screen Preview, and the film resizes the window to the display's size in its place.
- The `PERF` line measures the live decoder and one tick, layer commit included, on a 60 Hz timer in the harness. Core Animation's compositing runs outside the main thread and is not in the number.
